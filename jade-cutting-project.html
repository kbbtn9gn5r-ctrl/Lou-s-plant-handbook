The Family Jade Plant

A living family story, the June 29 pruning, and the 2026 propagation project.

Passed through the family

Lou’s mother cared for the original jade plant and later passed the plant itself to Lou’s sister-in-law, Augie.

Augie shared a cutting with her daughter.

Augie’s daughter later shared a cutting with Lou’s sister, Sue.

Sue gave Lou a cutting, which grew into the mature family jade documented in this project.

This plant is more than a houseplant—it is a living connection across generations. New plants propagated from it will continue that tradition by being shared with family, friends, and the local community.

June 29, 2026 pruning record

The mature jade was pruned because it had become extremely top-heavy and leaned against the cabinet for support. The pruning reduced weight, improved balance, and encouraged new branching. Healthy stems and leaves removed during pruning were saved for propagation.

Recovery update · August 20, 2026

After the June 29 pruning and later repotting, the mature jade now stands upright by itself without needing to lean against the cabinet. Healthy new growth is visible at several pruning points, and the plant is substantially better balanced.

Mature jade

Let the plant continue to grow and strengthen for now rather than pruning again immediately. Water deeply only after the potting mix has dried well and continue taking comparison photos.

New cuttings and pups

Use bright indirect light at first. Avoid frequent watering; unrooted jade pieces rot more easily than they dry out.

Next review

Reassess later for selective trimming only if new growth begins pulling the plant out of balance again.

Propagation update

The cuttings were allowed to callus before planting. Pups and cuttings were then placed in a fast-draining cactus or succulent mix. Rooting compound was not required. The newly planted pieces should remain on the dry side while roots begin forming.
