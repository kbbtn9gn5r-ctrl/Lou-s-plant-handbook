🎄 Boomer and Trixie and the Night They Caught Santa 🎅🐾

It was Christmas Eve, and snow was falling softly outside Lou and Peg’s house in Maine.

Inside, the Christmas tree sparkled, the stockings were hanging, and two very excited little dogs were supposed to be sleeping.

Trixie, the sweet little white dog, was curled up on her favorite blanket.

Boomer, the mischievous brown dog, had other plans.

“Psst! Trixie! Wake up!”

Trixie opened one eye. “What NOW, Boomer?”

“I heard Santa's coming tonight! And I’m going to catch him!”

“Oh, Boomer,” Trixie sighed. “You’re going to get us BOTH in trouble!”

But Boomer was already dragging his blanket behind the Christmas tree.

“I’m setting a trap!”

Unfortunately, Boomer’s idea of a trap involved three dog biscuits, one of Lou’s slippers, and a piece of ribbon he had stolen from under the tree.

Trixie shook her head. “That’s not a trap. That’s a mess!”

Just then, they heard something outside.

JINGLE! JINGLE! JINGLE!

Boomer’s ears shot straight up.

“SANTA!”

He raced toward the window so fast that he slid across the kitchen floor and landed nose-first in Peg’s Christmas slippers.

Trixie followed more carefully.

And there, standing in the snowy yard, was a great big sleigh with EIGHT REINDEER!

“HO! HO! HO!”

Boomer nearly fainted.

Santa Claus was climbing down the chimney!

Well, Boomer wasn't about to let THAT happen without introducing himself.

He raced into the living room, barking at the top of his lungs.

“WOOF! WOOF! WOOF!”

Santa froze halfway through putting presents under the tree.

“Well, hello there, little fellow!”

Boomer stopped dead in his tracks.

He had never seen a man with such an enormous beard.

Naturally, he decided to investigate.

He stuck his entire little nose into Santa’s beard.

“HO! HO! HO! That tickles!”

Then Boomer spotted something even more exciting.

Santa’s big red sack!

Before anyone could stop him, Boomer jumped right inside.

“BOOMER!” Trixie whispered. “GET OUT OF THERE!”

But it was too late.

Santa picked up his sack, slung it over his shoulder, and headed for the chimney.

And inside that sack was one very surprised little brown dog!

“WAIT!” Trixie barked.

Santa stopped.

“What’s the matter, little lady?”

Trixie pointed her nose toward the sack.

Suddenly, a little brown head popped out between a teddy bear and a toy fire truck.

“WOOF!”

Santa burst out laughing.

“Well, goodness gracious! I almost delivered you to the North Pole!”

Boomer thought that sounded like a WONDERFUL idea.

Trixie did not.

“BOOMER! YOU GET BACK HERE RIGHT NOW!”

Santa carefully lifted Boomer out of the sack and set him beside Trixie.

“You two remind me of a couple of elves I know. One gets into trouble, and the other has to get him out!”

Trixie gave Santa her very best innocent look.

Boomer wagged his tail.

Then Santa reached deep into his sack.

“I believe I have something special for you two.”

He pulled out two beautifully wrapped presents.

One contained a soft, fluffy white blanket for Trixie.

The other contained a brand-new squeaky reindeer for Boomer.

SQUEAK! SQUEAK! SQUEAK!

Boomer was in heaven.

But then Santa leaned down and whispered, “Now, I need you two to do me a very important favor.”

Trixie sat up straight.

Boomer dropped his reindeer.

“Tomorrow morning, when Taylor, Lillian, Carter, and Jacoby come to celebrate Christmas, I want you to help spread some Christmas magic!”

Trixie wagged her tail proudly.

Boomer immediately grabbed Santa’s mitten and ran away with it.

“BOOMER!” cried Trixie.

Santa laughed so hard that his belly shook.

“HO! HO! HO! I think that little fellow has enough Christmas spirit for the whole North Pole!”

Finally, Santa retrieved his mitten, climbed back up the chimney, and disappeared into the snowy night.

The two little dogs watched from the window as the reindeer lifted the sleigh into the sky.

“MERRY CHRISTMAS, BOOMER AND TRIXIE!”

And then Santa was gone.

The next morning, Lou and Peg came downstairs to find Trixie sleeping peacefully on her new white blanket.

Boomer, however, was sitting proudly beside the Christmas tree.

Surrounding him were shredded wrapping paper, three stolen dog biscuits, one missing slipper, and a squeaky reindeer that had somehow lost an antler.

Lou looked at Peg.

Peg looked at Lou.

And Trixie opened one eye with that familiar expression.

See? I TOLD you he was going to get in trouble!

But there was one more surprise.

Hanging from the Christmas tree was a little red mitten with a handwritten note.

It read:

“Dear Lou and Peg,

Please keep an eye on Boomer.

He has officially been placed on the North Pole’s MOST MISCHIEVOUS list.

Trixie, on the other hand, has earned a lifetime supply of Christmas cookies.

P.S. Boomer, I WANT MY MITTEN BACK!

Love,
Santa Claus.”

And somewhere high above the snowy woods of Maine, Rudolph turned to Santa and asked:

“Are we going back to Lou and Peg’s house next year?”

Santa smiled.

“Of course we are!”

Then he checked his sack.

“WAIT A MINUTE! WHERE’S MY OTHER MITTEN?”

Back at the house, Boomer was sleeping soundly.

With a big red mitten tucked underneath his blanket.

And Trixie?

She wasn't telling ANYBODY.

THE END. 🎄🐾❤️
