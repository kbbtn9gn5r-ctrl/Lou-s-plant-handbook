Hardneck Garlic

Complete Maine-season guidance from fall planting through curing and storage.

Planning & planting

Best time: Plant hardneck garlic in fall, usually after a few light frosts but before the ground freezes. Choose large, healthy cloves and plant each clove point-up about 2–3 inches deep and roughly 6 inches apart.

Use loose, well-drained soil enriched with compost. Avoid low areas that stay wet.

Winter protection

After planting, apply about 4–6 inches of clean straw or shredded leaves. Mulch reduces freeze-thaw movement and protects roots. Pull thick mulch back slightly in spring as shoots emerge.

Spring growth

Keep the bed weed-free and evenly moist. Feed in early spring with compost or a nitrogen-forward organic fertilizer, then stop high-nitrogen feeding as bulbs begin swelling.

Scapes

Hardneck garlic produces curling scapes. Cut them after they make one curl so the plant directs more energy into bulb growth. Scapes are edible.

Watering

Provide about 1 inch of water weekly when rainfall is lacking. Reduce watering as lower leaves begin browning, and stop watering roughly one to two weeks before harvest.

Harvest

Harvest when several lower leaves are brown but five or six upper leaves remain green. Loosen bulbs with a fork; do not pull hard by the stems.

Curing & storage

Cure whole plants in shade with excellent airflow for about two to three weeks. Do not wash bulbs. When necks and wrappers are dry, trim roots and stems and store in a cool, dry, ventilated place.

Fall cleanup & rotation

Remove diseased debris and rotate garlic and other alliums to a different bed for three to four years when possible. Do not compost material showing serious rot.
