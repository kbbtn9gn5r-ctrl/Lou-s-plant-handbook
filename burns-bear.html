Woodland Habitat Tree Carving

Six days of carving and finishing transformed a preserved tree into a bald eagle, black bear, birds, owl, squirrel, mouse, and other woodland details.

From tree to woodland habitat

A large portion of the original tree was intentionally preserved so it could begin a second life as art. Chainsaw artist Dan Burns of Burns Bears, Augusta, Maine, transformed the standing trunk into a three-dimensional wildlife scene led by a bald eagle and climbing black bear.

The direct link opens only this carving project, while the project also remains featured on Lou’s Garden Guide.

Project status: Complete · August 2026
Day 1

First cuts and rough shaping

Scaffolding, guide cuts, and major wood removal established the overall proportions. By the end of the day, the eagle’s head, body, wings, and supporting structure were recognizable.

Day 2

The wildlife scene emerges

The eagle gained depth and layered feathers while smaller woodland animals began appearing around the trunk, making the sculpture readable from multiple sides.

Day 3

Detail, depth, and character

Feather work became cleaner, the eagle’s expression strengthened, and the bear, owl, and smaller wildlife became more distinct.

Day 4

Refining the wildlife

The eagle, bear, owl, and smaller animals received significant refinement, with deeper texture and clearer separation from the original trunk.

Day 5 · Rain Day

Detail work through a day of rain

Rain fell throughout Day 5, but the carving continued. Dan worked from the scaffolding and around protective tarps, refining the black bear, eagle, owl, smaller birds, trunk texture, and the many branches that support the wildlife. The day marked a clear transition from rough shaping to the finer work that gives each animal its individual character.

Day 5: Work continued all day despite the rain. These photographs preserve both the difficult weather and the steady progress that brought the sculpture close to its final form.
Day 6 · Painting, Preservation & Completion

Color, protective finish, and the final reveal

Day 6 was devoted to bringing the carving to life and protecting the finished wood. Dan Burns added final color to the bald eagle, black bear, owl, cardinals, bluebird and other woodland details, then applied protective wood preservative across the sculpture. These photographs document the full finishing process before the scaffolding came down.

The Finished Sculpture

A permanent woodland habitat in the garden

After six working days, the scaffolding and tarps came down to reveal the completed sculpture. Dan Burns of Burns Bears, Augusta, Maine, transformed the preserved trunk into a permanent wildlife landmark overlooking the garden and surrounding Maine landscape.
