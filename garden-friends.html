Garden Friends

Birds, pollinators, amphibians, and other wildlife that share and support Lou’s garden.

A garden is a living community

These welcome visitors pollinate flowers, help control pests, and bring movement and song to the garden. Photographed in Lou’s gardens in Wayne, Maine.

New August 18, 2026: hummingbird moth, bumble bee on marigolds, milkweed tussock moth caterpillars, and multiple pollinators sharing a squash blossom.

Observed August 18, 2026 · Pollinator

Hummingbird Moth

A clearwing-type hummingbird moth visiting the pink phlox. It hovers in front of flowers and uses its long proboscis to reach nectar, moving pollen as it travels from bloom to bloom.

A fast-moving daytime moth that can look remarkably like a tiny hummingbird.
Observed August 18, 2026 · Milkweed insect

Milkweed Tussock Moth Caterpillars

These colorful caterpillars were photographed feeding together on milkweed. They are part of the native milkweed insect community, so they are recorded here under Garden Friends rather than as a general garden pest.

Their feeding can make leaves look rough, but they belong to the same milkweed habitat that supports many garden insects.
Observed August 18, 2026 · Vegetable garden

Squash Blossom Pollinators

Several bees were photographed sharing a large squash or pumpkin blossom. This kind of active flower visitation is exactly what the vine crops need for good pollen transfer and fruit set.

A busy squash blossom is a very good sign for the garden.
Observed August 1, 2026

American Toad

This healthy adult toad was found resting in the vegetable-garden mulch. Toads are valuable natural pest-control partners that consume many insects, slugs, beetles, and caterpillars.

Finding this toad reminds us that nature provides some of the garden’s best pest control.
Pollinator · Updated August 18, 2026

Bumble Bee

Bumble bees are especially important in Maine because they work in cool weather and carry large amounts of pollen. Their buzz-pollination behavior is particularly useful for blueberries and tomatoes. On August 18, another bumble bee was photographed working the marigolds.

Every bumble bee is a welcome visitor, helping produce food that can be shared with family, friends, and neighbors.
Young garden visitor

Fledgling Bird

A recently fledged young bird resting safely on the garden fence while learning to fly and forage. Mature trees, shrubs, water, and safe perches help young birds thrive.

Summer 2026

Baltimore Oriole

A Baltimore Oriole visiting Lou’s bird-feeding area. Orioles eat insects and caterpillars, enjoy orange halves and small amounts of grape jelly, and add brilliant color and song to the summer garden.

Website note: A still image from Lou’s original video is shown here so the website remains quick and easy to upload. The full video remains in Lou’s personal archive.

The return of the Baltimore Oriole is one of the seasonal highlights of the garden.
