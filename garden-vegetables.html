Vegetable Garden

Named crops, harvest notes, and seasonal care.

Tomatoes

Seed to transplant

  • Start indoors 6–8 weeks before the last expected frost.
  • Transplant after frost danger passes and soil has warmed; harden plants for 7–10 days.
  • Plant deeply, leaving the top several sets of leaves above soil. Space 24–36 inches apart.

Season care

  • Full sun, rich well-drained soil, and 1–1½ inches of water weekly.
  • Mulch after soil warms and support with sturdy cages or stakes.
  • Use a tomato fertilizer according to label directions after fruit set; avoid excess nitrogen.
  • Tie new growth to supports and remove leaves touching the soil.
  • Water at soil level and keep moisture even to reduce cracking and blossom-end rot.
  • Watch for hornworms, leaf spots, early blight, and late blight.
  • Harvest when fruit has full color and gives slightly to gentle pressure.
  • Before frost, pick mature green fruit to ripen indoors.
  • Remove and discard diseased vines; compost only healthy material. Clean cages and stakes.

Borlund and Red Pontiac Potatoes

  • Plant certified seed potatoes in cool spring soil, about 3–4 inches deep and 10–12 inches apart.
  • Hill soil or mulch around stems as plants reach 6–8 inches, repeating until tubers are protected from light.
  • Keep evenly moist, especially from flowering through tuber development.
  • Watch for Colorado potato beetles, flea beetles, and blight.
  • Avoid exposing tubers to sunlight, which causes greening.
  • Stop heavy watering as tops naturally yellow.
  • Harvest new potatoes after flowering; harvest storage potatoes 2–3 weeks after vines die back.
  • Cure in a dark, ventilated place for about 1–2 weeks, then store cool and dark.
  • Remove volunteer tubers and diseased foliage from the bed.

King of the North Pepper

  • Start indoors 8–10 weeks before the last frost with warm soil, ideally 75–85°F.
  • Transplant only after nights are reliably above about 50°F; space 18–24 inches apart.
  • Provide full sun, warm soil, steady moisture, and light support as fruit develops.
  • Mulch after the soil warms. Avoid high-nitrogen feeding after flowering begins.
  • Watch for aphids, flea beetles, and sunscald.
  • Do not allow plants to wilt repeatedly, which can reduce fruit set.
  • Pick green or allow fruit to color fully for sweeter flavor.
  • Harvest before frost; peppers can continue ripening indoors.
  • Remove plants after frost and clean supports.

Hubbard Squash and Cucumbers

  • Direct sow after frost when soil is warm, or start indoors 3–4 weeks early in biodegradable pots.
  • Give vines full sun, rich soil, and ample room; hill or row spacing depends on variety.
  • Water deeply at soil level and mulch to keep fruit clean and moisture steady.
  • Check frequently for cucumber beetles, squash bug eggs, and squash vine borer damage.
  • Protect young plants with row cover, removing it when female flowers appear for pollination.
  • Guide vines away from paths and lift large fruit onto boards or straw.
  • Harvest cucumbers young and often. Harvest Hubbard squash when rind is hard and stem is corky, before a hard frost.
  • Cure winter squash in a warm, dry place, then store cool and dry.
  • Remove vines and fruit debris to reduce overwintering pests.

Detroit Beets

  • Direct sow in early spring and again in late summer for fall harvest.
  • Sow ½ inch deep; thin seedlings to 3–4 inches because each seed cluster can produce several plants.
  • Grow in full sun to light shade with loose, evenly moist soil.
  • Keep weeds down and avoid letting soil dry hard.
  • Use removed thinnings as beet greens.
  • Watch for leaf miners and flea beetles.
  • Harvest roots around 1½–3 inches across, or at the size you prefer.
  • Cut tops to about 1 inch before storage; refrigerate or store in cool, humid conditions.
  • Remove old leaves and roots after the final harvest.

Hardneck Garlic

Fall planting

  • Plant hardneck cloves in fall, usually 4–6 weeks before the ground freezes.
  • Separate bulbs just before planting. Set cloves pointed end up, about 2–3 inches deep and 6 inches apart.
  • Use well-drained soil in full sun and cover with 4–6 inches of clean straw mulch for winter.

Spring and summer

  • Pull mulch back slightly if spring soil stays wet, then retain enough to suppress weeds.
  • Side-dress with nitrogen early in spring; stop fertilizing as bulbs begin swelling.
  • Provide about 1 inch of water weekly, then reduce and stop watering roughly 1–2 weeks before harvest.
  • Keep beds weed-free because garlic competes poorly.
  • Remove hardneck scapes after they curl once or twice; use them in cooking.
  • Watch for basal rot, white rot, onion maggots, and poor drainage.
  • Harvest when several lower leaves are brown but about five or six upper leaves remain green.
  • Lift bulbs gently; do not wash. Cure in shade with strong airflow for 2–3 weeks.
  • Trim roots and tops after curing, save the healthiest bulbs for fall planting, and clean the bed.

Sunflowers

  • Direct sow after frost, 1 inch deep, in full sun. Space according to mature size.
  • Choose a site protected from strong wind or stake tall varieties early.
  • Water regularly during establishment; mature plants tolerate brief dry periods.
  • Protect seedlings from birds and rodents.
  • Watch for stem borers, beetles, and fungal leaf spots.
  • Support heavy heads if stems begin leaning.
  • For seed, let heads mature until backs turn yellow-brown and seeds are plump.
  • Cover heads with breathable mesh if birds are taking seeds.
  • Leave some heads for wildlife or cut and dry them; remove stalks after frost.
