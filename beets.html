Detroit Beets

Complete guidance from direct sowing through harvest, storage, and fall cleanup.

Seed starting & sowing

Beets are usually direct-sown. Begin in early spring as soon as soil can be worked, then sow additional short rows every two to three weeks for a longer harvest. A late-summer sowing can provide fall roots.

Planting depth & spacing

Sow seed clusters about 1/2 inch deep and 1–2 inches apart in rows 12–18 inches apart. Each “seed” can produce several seedlings, so thinning is essential.

Thinning

When seedlings are a few inches tall, thin to roughly 3–4 inches apart for full-size roots. Young thinnings are edible as beet greens.

Soil & feeding

Use loose, stone-free soil with compost. Keep soil near neutral and avoid excessive nitrogen, which produces large tops and small roots.

Watering & mulch

Keep moisture steady—about 1 inch per week. Uneven watering can make roots tough or cause cracking. A light mulch helps retain moisture and suppress weeds.

Pests & problems

Watch for leaf miners, flea beetles, and cercospora leaf spot. Remove badly affected leaves and keep foliage dry by watering at soil level.

Harvest

Harvest baby beets at 1–2 inches across or mature roots around 2–3 inches. Pull after rain or loosen with a fork. Greens can be harvested lightly while roots develop.

Storage & fall cleanup

Cut tops to about 1 inch without cutting the crown. Store unwashed roots in a perforated bag in a cold refrigerator or packed in slightly damp sand in a cool cellar. Remove crop debris and rotate away from the same bed next year.
