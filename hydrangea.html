Lou’s Garden Guide

Hydrangea

Season-long planting, maintenance, pruning, and winter care.

Complete Care Guide

  • Plant in spring or early fall in moisture-retentive, well-drained soil.
  • Morning sun with afternoon shade is ideal in many Maine sites; protect from hot, drying wind.
  • Water deeply during establishment and maintain 2–3 inches of mulch without piling it against stems.
  • Water during dry periods, especially while buds and flowers are developing.
  • Apply compost in spring; use fertilizer sparingly because excess nitrogen can reduce bloom.
  • Remove dead, damaged, or crossing stems and monitor for leaf spot, mildew, and deer browsing.

Pruning depends on the hydrangea type. Until the exact cultivar is confirmed, avoid severe fall or spring pruning.

  • Old-wood bloomers such as many bigleaf and oakleaf hydrangeas: prune just after flowering.
  • New-wood bloomers such as smooth and panicle hydrangeas: prune in late winter or early spring.
  • Dead wood can be removed whenever it is clearly lifeless.
  • Stop fertilizing by midsummer so stems harden before winter.
  • Leave dried flower heads for winter interest or remove them carefully without cutting live buds.
  • In exposed sites, protect the root zone with mulch after the ground freezes.
