Flowers

Named perennials and seasonal bloom progression.

Hosta

  • Plant in spring or early fall in partial to full shade, in rich soil that stays evenly moist but drains well.
  • Space for mature width and water deeply during establishment.
  • Divide crowded clumps in spring or early fall.
  • Water during dry periods and mulch lightly without covering crowns.
  • Remove damaged leaves and spent flower stalks as desired.
  • Watch for slugs, snails, deer, and vole damage.
  • Allow foliage to yellow after frost, then cut it to the ground and remove debris.
  • Refresh mulch after the ground cools, keeping it away from crowns.

Petunias

  • Start seed indoors 10–12 weeks before the last frost or plant purchased transplants after frost.
  • Grow in full sun with well-drained potting mix or garden soil.
  • Space plants for airflow and water when the top inch begins to dry.
  • Feed regularly during active bloom according to the fertilizer label.
  • Deadhead seed-forming types and trim leggy stems by one-third to renew growth.
  • Watch for aphids, budworms, and gray mold.
  • Petunias are annuals in Maine. Remove after frost and discard diseased material.
  • Clean containers before storing or replanting.

Liatris

  • Plant corms in spring in full sun and well-drained soil, about 2–4 inches deep.
  • Space 12–15 inches apart and avoid soggy winter soil.
  • Water during establishment; mature plants are moderately drought tolerant.
  • Stake only if plants lean. Deadhead for tidiness or leave seed for birds.
  • Cut stems after they brown in fall or leave until early spring for winter interest.
  • Divide crowded clumps every few years in spring.

Lilies

  • Plant bulbs in spring or fall in full sun to light shade, about three times as deep as the bulb height.
  • Keep roots cool with mulch or neighboring plants while stems receive sun.
  • Remove spent flowers but leave green stems and leaves until they yellow naturally.
  • Watch for lily leaf beetles and hand-pick adults, larvae, and eggs.
  • Stake tall stems before bloom if needed.
  • Cut yellowed stems to the ground in fall and remove beetle-infested debris.
  • Mark bulb locations before winter so they are not disturbed in spring.

Garden Phlox

  • Plant in full sun to light afternoon shade with fertile, moisture-retentive soil and good airflow.
  • Space generously to reduce powdery mildew.
  • Pinch some stems in early June for shorter plants and staggered bloom.
  • Water at soil level and deadhead faded clusters.
  • Thin crowded stems in spring and monitor for powdery mildew and spider mites.
  • Divide every 3–4 years if flowering declines.
  • Cut stems to the ground after frost and remove diseased foliage rather than composting it.
  • Apply a light mulch after the ground freezes.

Sedum

  • Plant in full sun and sharply drained soil. Avoid rich soil that encourages floppy growth.
  • Divide in spring when clumps become crowded.
  • Water sparingly after establishment and avoid heavy fertilizer.
  • Pinch tall types in late spring if a shorter, sturdier plant is desired.
  • Leave dried flower heads for winter interest and birds, then cut back in early spring.
  • Remove soft or rotted stems caused by poor drainage.

Wild Ginger

  • Plant in partial to full shade in humus-rich, evenly moist, well-drained soil.
  • Use as a woodland groundcover beneath trees and shrubs. Plants spread slowly by rhizomes.
  • Water regularly during the first growing season and during prolonged drought.
  • Top-dress with leaf mold or compost in spring.
  • Keep aggressive weeds out while the patch fills in.
  • Usually needs little pruning; remove damaged leaves as needed.
  • Leave healthy fallen leaves as natural winter mulch or apply a thin layer of shredded leaves.
  • Remove only diseased or badly damaged foliage in spring before new growth expands.
