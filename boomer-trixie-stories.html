Boomer & Trixie Adventures

Real dogs. Real mischief. Real life. Boomer is the brown little troublemaker. Trixie is the white, sensible one who usually sees the trouble coming.

The rule of the series: Boomer usually has an idea. Trixie usually gives him the look that says, “Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.” Lou and Peg are never very far behind.

1. The Flower Pot Caper

Boomer finds a new digging spot, and Trixie tries to keep him out of trouble.

It started with one perfectly innocent flower pot sitting beside the garden.

At least, it was innocent until Boomer noticed the soft, dark soil inside it.

Boomer sniffed the rim. Then he sniffed the dirt. Then he looked over at Trixie.

Trixie was sitting a few feet away, watching him carefully. She knew that look.

“Boomer,” she warned, “you’re gonna get in trouble.”

Boomer put one paw into the pot.

Trixie stared at him.

Boomer put in the other paw. A little dirt flew over the side.

“I’m just checking it,” Boomer said.

Then he checked it harder.

Dirt flew. Flowers tipped. The pot rolled onto its side, and Boomer emerged with soil on his paws, his nose, and somehow the top of his head.

That was exactly when Lou came around the corner.

Lou stopped. Boomer stopped. Trixie did not move at all.

“Boomer...” Lou said.

Boomer looked at Trixie as if she might have an explanation ready.

Trixie gave him her best innocent look. “I told you.”

Peg came outside and saw the pot. She looked at Boomer’s muddy face and tried very hard not to laugh.

Lou set the pot upright and swept the soil back together. Boomer sat beside him, watching like an experienced garden supervisor.

Trixie sat on the clean side of the walkway.

When the job was finished, Lou pointed at the flower pot. “We leave that alone.”

Boomer nodded.

For almost a whole minute.

Then he noticed a second flower pot.

Trixie sighed. “Here we go again.”

2. A Ride to Remember

Boomer has a plan, of course, and Trixie goes along for the adventure.

When Lou picked up the car keys, Boomer knew immediately what they meant.

“Ride!”

He raced to the door. Trixie followed at a more reasonable speed.

“You don’t even know where we’re going,” she said.

“Doesn’t matter,” Boomer answered. “We’re going somewhere.”

They climbed into the car with Lou and Peg. Trixie settled into her spot. Boomer went directly to the window.

The road wound past fields, woods, and glimpses of blue Maine water. Every few seconds Boomer discovered something that apparently required immediate investigation.

A mailbox. A tractor. A dog in another car. A suspicious-looking crow.

Trixie watched him bounce from one side to the other.

“Boomer, sit down.”

Then they stopped at a little overlook by the lake.

Lou opened the door and clipped on their leashes. Boomer stepped out like an explorer arriving on a brand-new continent.

He sniffed one rock, two bushes, three pine needles, and a patch of grass that was clearly the most interesting grass in Maine.

Trixie simply stood in the breeze and looked across the water.

For once, Boomer became quiet. He walked over and sat beside her.

“Pretty good ride,” Trixie said.

Boomer leaned against her. “I knew where we were going.”

Trixie looked at him. “No, you didn’t.”

Back in the car, both dogs curled up together and slept nearly all the way home.

Lou glanced back at them. “They had a good day.”

Peg smiled. “They always do.”

Boomer opened one eye just long enough to make sure everyone understood that the trip had been his idea.

3. The Peanut Butter Problem

Some things are just too tempting for Boomer. Trixie gives him that look.

There are certain sounds every dog recognizes.

The crinkle of a treat bag. The refrigerator door. And, in Boomer’s opinion, the most important sound of all: the lid coming off the peanut butter jar.

Peg was making a snack in the kitchen when the jar slipped from the counter. It landed safely on the floor, but the lid rolled away.

Boomer was there before the lid stopped moving.

Trixie arrived one second later.

“Boomer,” she said.

He stared at the open jar.

“Don’t.”

Boomer looked at Trixie, then at the peanut butter, then toward the hallway to see whether Lou was coming.

He lowered his nose.

“You’re gonna get in trouble.”

One tiny lick became two. Two became a heroic attempt to reach the bottom of the jar.

When Lou walked into the kitchen, Boomer’s entire nose was covered in peanut butter.

Trixie sat several feet away, spotless and dignified.

Lou folded his arms. “Boomer?”

Boomer tried to look innocent. Unfortunately, his tongue kept licking his own face.

Peg picked up the jar and laughed. “I think the evidence is pretty strong.”

Trixie gave Boomer a long look.

Boomer finally sat down. “It fell on the floor.”

“That does not make it yours,” Trixie said.

Lou cleaned Boomer’s face and put the jar away.

Later, both dogs received a proper little treat.

Boomer ate his and looked toward the kitchen counter.

Trixie moved closer and whispered, “Don’t even think about it.”

4. The Stolen Blanket

A cozy blanket, a sneaky Boomer, and a patient Trixie.

Trixie had a favorite blanket.

It was soft, warm, and folded into the perfect little nest on the couch.

One afternoon Trixie walked into the room and discovered a problem.

Boomer was in it.

Not beside it. Not partly on it. Completely, comfortably, unmistakably in the middle of her blanket.

Trixie stopped and stared.

Boomer opened one eye.

“Nice blanket,” he said.

Trixie gave him the look.

“That is my spot.”

Boomer stretched, somehow taking up even more room.

“There’s space.”

There was not space.

Trixie tried the left side. Boomer rolled left. She tried the right side. Boomer rolled right.

Finally, Trixie sat on the floor and looked toward Lou.

Lou looked at Boomer. “You stole Trixie’s blanket.”

Boomer wagged his tail.

Peg came in and unfolded the blanket so it covered the whole couch cushion.

“There. Now you can share.”

Trixie climbed up and settled on one end. Boomer stayed on the other.

For five minutes, everything was peaceful.

Then Boomer slowly scooted closer.

Trixie opened one eye.

Boomer scooted again until his back touched hers.

Trixie considered complaining. Instead, she rested her head against him.

By the time Lou looked over again, both dogs were asleep together.

The blanket, apparently, belonged to both of them now.

5. Fall in Maine

Leaves, laughs, and a little trouble. Boomer can’t resist, and Trixie keeps him in line.

Fall had arrived in Maine, and the yard was covered in red, orange, and yellow leaves.

Lou had spent the morning raking them into a very respectable pile.

Boomer spent the morning watching.

Trixie knew exactly what he was thinking.

“Boomer, don’t.”

Boomer looked at the leaf pile. Then at Trixie. Then at the leaf pile again.

He backed up.

“Boomer.”

He ran.

WHOOSH!

The beautiful pile exploded in every direction. Leaves flew over Boomer’s head, across the grass, and onto Trixie.

Boomer popped out of the middle with a red leaf stuck to his ear.

He looked extremely pleased with himself.

Trixie looked extremely unimpressed.

Lou stared at what remained of his pile.

“Boomer...”

Peg laughed from the porch.

Then a gust of wind swept through the yard and sent another shower of leaves spinning around both dogs.

Trixie tried to remain dignified. She really did.

But one leaf skittered past her nose.

She chased it.

That was all the permission Boomer needed.

Soon both dogs were racing through the leaves, circling the yard and jumping through every pile they could find.

Lou finally gave up on raking for a while.

He and Peg watched Boomer and Trixie play until the dogs were tired enough to flop down together in the grass.

Boomer still had the leaf on his ear.

Trixie looked at him. “You know Lou has to rake all of that again.”

Boomer wagged his tail. “Good. Then we get another pile tomorrow.”

6. Boomer & Trixie and the Tuesday Lunch Adventure

A Tuesday family visit brings cheeseburgers, games, trucks, hummingbirds, a garden walk, and just enough Boomer-and-Trixie trouble.

Every Tuesday, Boomer and Trixie seem to know before anyone tells them that company is coming for lunch.

This Tuesday was no different.

Peg was in the kitchen getting everything ready. She had planned a healthy lunch, made sure there was plenty of organic fruit, and added one very important item to the menu: cheeseburgers.

Lillian and Carter loved cheeseburgers.

Lou wandered into the kitchen at exactly the right time.

“Are we eating yet?” he asked.

Peg looked at him. “Lou, they’re not even here yet.”

“I was just checking.”

Boomer sat near Lou as if he were also just checking.

Trixie looked at the two of them and seemed to think they were very much alike.

Then a car door closed outside.

Boomer sprang to his feet.

Trixie hurried toward the door.

Lillian came in first with Carter right behind her.

Boomer started bouncing. Trixie started wiggling. Lillian laughed and Carter laughed, which only made both dogs more excited.

“Boomer, Trixie, settle down,” Lou said.

Neither dog settled down.

Boomer hurried one way. Trixie hurried the other. Then they traded places as if being told to settle down meant they should run a little faster.

Trixie finally stopped and looked at Boomer.

Her expression said, “Boomer, we’re gonna get in trouble.”

A moment later Taylor came in with little Jacoby.

Now the whole Tuesday lunch crew was together.

After all the greetings, Lillian found some games to play. Carter headed for his trucks.

“Vroom! Vroom!”

One truck rolled across the floor.

Boomer watched it.

Trixie watched Boomer.

The truck rolled past again.

Boomer took one step toward it.

Trixie gave him the look.

“Don’t.”

Boomer stopped.

For about three seconds.

Then Carter sent the truck racing across the room and Boomer raced after it.

“Boomer!” Lou called.

Boomer stopped beside the truck and looked back as though he had absolutely no idea how he had gotten there.

Carter giggled.

Trixie sat nearby looking very innocent.

Soon Peg called everyone to lunch.

There were cheeseburgers for Lillian and Carter, along with the healthy foods Peg had prepared and a colorful bowl of organic fruit.

Lou was ready.

Boomer was also ready.

Trixie took her usual place nearby.

Boomer sat where he could keep one eye on the table.

“No begging,” Lou reminded him.

Boomer sat up straighter and tried to look as if begging had never crossed his mind.

Trixie knew better.

After lunch, Lou helped Peg with the dishes while Taylor and the children settled back in.

Then Lillian noticed movement outside the window.

“Look at the birds!”

Everyone gathered near the window.

There were birds moving through the yard, and then a hummingbird appeared, hovering and darting so quickly that everyone tried to keep track of where it would go next.

Lillian watched closely. Carter watched too.

Even Boomer and Trixie stared through the glass.

The hummingbird zipped left.

Boomer’s head turned left.

It zipped right.

Boomer’s head turned right.

Trixie looked at him.

“Don’t get any ideas.”

When the hummingbird finally disappeared, Lou asked, “Who wants to see what’s ready in the garden?”

Lillian and Carter were ready immediately.

Outside they went, with Lillian and Carter walking along with Boomer and Trixie toward the garden.

Taylor came along with Jacoby, and Lou and Peg followed.

The garden was full of things to inspect.

Lillian looked carefully to see what might be ready to eat. Carter checked the plants from his own point of view, especially anything growing close to the ground.

Lou showed them what was ready and what still needed more time. Peg helped decide what should come back to the house.

Boomer sniffed along the edge of the garden.

Trixie stayed close to Lillian and Carter.

Then Boomer found something very interesting between two plants.

He pushed his nose in farther.

Trixie saw him.

“Boomer.”

Boomer kept sniffing.

“Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.”

A plant rustled.

Boomer jumped backward with a leaf stuck on top of his head.

Lillian laughed.

Carter laughed.

Lou looked over. “What are you doing now, Boomer?”

Boomer stood perfectly still with the leaf still on his head.

Trixie looked at him as if to say, “I told you.”

By the time everyone walked back to the house, it had been a very good Tuesday.

There had been cheeseburgers and organic fruit, games and trucks, birds and hummingbirds, a walk to the garden, and a little bit of trouble from two dogs who were simply too excited to have everyone visiting.

Back inside, Lillian returned to her games. Carter found his trucks. Taylor settled in with Jacoby. Peg finished putting things away, and Lou found a comfortable place to sit after helping with the dishes.

Boomer curled up nearby.

Trixie lay beside him.

For a few quiet moments, both dogs were completely still.

Then Boomer opened one eye.

“Do they come back next Tuesday?”

Trixie looked at him.

“Boomer. They come every Tuesday.”

Boomer’s tail thumped against the floor.

“Good.”

And just like that, he was already looking forward to the next Tuesday lunch adventure.
