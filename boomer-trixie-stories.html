Boomer & Trixie Adventures

Real dogs. Real mischief. Real life. Boomer is the brown little troublemaker. Trixie is the white, sensible one who usually sees the trouble coming.

The rule of the series: Boomer usually has an idea. Trixie usually gives him the look that says, “Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.” Lou and Peg are never very far behind.

1. The Flower Pot Caper

Boomer finds a new digging spot, and Trixie tries to keep him out of trouble.

It started with one perfectly innocent flower pot sitting beside the garden.

At least, it was innocent until Boomer noticed the soft, dark soil inside it.

Trixie saw the look on his face. “Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.”

Boomer put one paw into the pot, then the other. Dirt flew, flowers tipped, and the pot rolled onto its side.

That was exactly when Lou came around the corner.

Boomer froze with soil on his paws and nose. Trixie sat neatly nearby.

Peg came outside, tried not to laugh, and Lou put the flower pot back together.

When the job was finished, Lou pointed at the pot. “We leave that alone.”

Boomer nodded. For almost a whole minute.

Then he noticed a second flower pot.

Trixie sighed. “Here we go again.”

2. A Ride to Remember

Boomer convinces Trixie that a wagon ride is exactly what the day needs.

Boomer spotted the little red wagon and immediately decided it was meant for adventure.

He hopped in and looked at Trixie. “Come on.”

Trixie gave him the look. “This has trouble written all over it.”

But Lou was there to pull the wagon, so Trixie climbed in beside Boomer.

They rolled past the yard and garden, Boomer leaning this way and that to inspect every bird, leaf, and squirrel.

“Sit down,” Trixie warned.

Boomer finally did, and the two of them rode side by side like seasoned travelers.

By the time they came back, Boomer was certain it had been the greatest expedition in history.

Trixie simply smiled. It had been a pretty good ride after all.

3. The Peanut Butter Problem

Some things are just too tempting for Boomer. Trixie gives him that look.

Boomer knew the sound of the peanut butter jar immediately.

When the jar ended up within reach, he stared at it like it was treasure.

Trixie arrived behind him. “Boomer. Don’t.”

He looked at Trixie, then at the jar, then toward the hallway.

One little taste became enough evidence to cover his nose.

Lou walked in and folded his arms. “Boomer?”

Trixie sat several feet away, perfectly clean.

Peg laughed while Lou cleaned Boomer’s face.

Later both dogs received a proper little treat, and Trixie stayed close enough to make sure Boomer did not go back for seconds.

4. The Stolen Blanket

A cozy blanket, a sneaky Boomer, and a patient Trixie.

Trixie had a favorite blanket, soft and warm and folded into the perfect little nest.

One afternoon she discovered Boomer right in the middle of it.

“That is my spot,” Trixie said.

Boomer stretched and somehow took up even more room.

Peg unfolded the blanket so there was room for both dogs.

Trixie settled on one end. Boomer stayed on the other—for about five minutes.

Then he slowly scooted closer until his back touched hers.

Trixie considered complaining, but instead rested her head against him.

By the time Lou looked over, both dogs were asleep together.

5. Fall in Maine

Leaves, laughs, and a little trouble on a beautiful fall day in Maine.

Fall had arrived in Maine, and Lou had raked a beautiful pile of red, orange, and yellow leaves.

Boomer stared at it.

Trixie knew exactly what he was thinking. “Boomer, don’t.”

Boomer ran.

WHOOSH! Leaves flew everywhere.

Lou stared at what remained of his pile while Peg laughed from the porch.

Then a leaf skittered past Trixie’s nose, and even she could not resist chasing it.

Soon both dogs were racing through the leaves.

Boomer flopped down beside Trixie and wagged his tail. “If Lou rakes them again tomorrow, we can do it again.”

6. The Tuesday Lunch Adventure

Family, cheeseburgers, birds, the garden, and just enough Boomer-and-Trixie trouble.

Every Tuesday, Boomer and Trixie somehow know that company is coming for lunch.

This Tuesday, the visit fit right between Lillian’s and Carter’s gymnastics lessons.

The first ones through the door were Lillian and Carter.

Boomer raced to greet them, tail wagging so hard his whole body wiggled. Trixie hurried behind him, trying to remember that sensible dogs do not leap around the doorway.

A little later Taylor came in carrying Jacoby, and the excitement started all over again.

“Easy, Boomer. Easy, Trixie,” Lou reminded them.

They listened for about three seconds.

While everyone settled in, Lillian found some games and Carter went straight to his trucks.

One truck rolled across the floor. Boomer’s eyes followed it.

Trixie saw what he was thinking. “Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.”

Boomer chased it anyway. Carter laughed, Lou called Boomer back, and Trixie sat there looking very pleased that she had warned him.

Then Peg announced lunch.

She had prepared a healthy meal, including cheeseburgers—one of Lillian and Carter’s favorites—and plenty of organic fruit.

Lou was ready to eat before anyone else had even reached the table.

Boomer and Trixie took up their usual positions nearby, sitting quietly and hoping someone might accidentally drop something.

After lunch Lou helped Peg with the dishes while the children returned to their games and trucks.

Before long everyone gathered at the window to watch the birds outside. A hummingbird zipped into view, hovering for a moment before darting away.

Boomer’s head moved back and forth trying to follow it.

Trixie watched him carefully. “Don’t get any ideas.”

Then Lou asked, “Who wants to see what’s ready in the garden?”

Lillian and Carter were ready immediately.

Out they went, with Lillian and Carter walking beside Boomer and Trixie on the way to the garden. Taylor followed with Jacoby, and Peg and Lou came along too.

The children checked the vegetables and looked to see what might be ready to eat.

Boomer sniffed along the edge of the plants until his nose disappeared between two rows.

“Boomer,” Trixie warned.

A rustle came from the leaves. Boomer jumped backward with a leaf stuck on his head.

Lillian and Carter laughed.

Lou looked over. “What are you doing, Boomer?”

Boomer stood perfectly still, trying to look innocent.

Trixie looked at him. “You never listen.”

By the time everyone headed back to the house, the Tuesday visit had included gymnastics, cheeseburgers, organic fruit, games, trucks, birds, hummingbirds, a garden walk, and just enough dog trouble to make everyone laugh.

Lou helped finish up in the kitchen. Peg put the last things away. Taylor settled in with Jacoby. Lillian returned to her games and Carter found his trucks.

Boomer curled up beside Trixie.

“That was a good Tuesday,” he said.

Trixie agreed.

Boomer lifted his head. “Do you think they’ll come back next Tuesday?”

Trixie stared at him. “Boomer. They come every Tuesday.”

Boomer’s tail thumped the floor.

“Good,” he said. “I’ll be ready for lunch.”
