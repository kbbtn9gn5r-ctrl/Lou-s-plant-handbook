Boomer & Trixie Adventures

A growing collection of family stories starring Boomer, the lovable little troublemaker, and Trixie, the sensible innocent one who can usually see the trouble coming.

The rule of the series: Boomer usually has an idea. Trixie usually gives him the look that says, “Boomer, you’re gonna get in trouble.” Lou and Peg are never very far behind.

The Garden Patrol

Boomer & Trixie Adventure · September 6, 2026

Once upon a time, Boomer and Trixie were sitting by the window, keeping a very serious watch over the backyard.

Boomer suddenly stood up.

“TRIXIE!”

Trixie barely opened one eye. “What?”

“There’s something in the garden.”

Trixie jumped up. This was serious. The garden was their territory. Well… technically it was Lou and Peg’s garden, but Boomer considered himself Head of Security.

They raced to the door. Lou let them outside, and off they went.

Boomer charged ahead, nose to the ground. Trixie followed more carefully. They passed the blueberries, inspected the vegetables, and stopped beside the sunflowers.

Something moved.

Boomer froze. Trixie froze.

A tiny rabbit popped out.

“INTRUDER!” Boomer barked.

The rabbit took off. Boomer chased the rabbit. Trixie chased Boomer. And Lou chased both of them.

“BOOMER! TRIXIE!”

The rabbit slipped safely through the fence.

Boomer skidded to a stop and stared through the opening. Trixie walked up beside him.

“We almost had him,” Boomer said.

Trixie looked at him. “Boomer, you weren't even close.”

Then they heard another sound.

Crunch. Crunch.

Boomer slowly turned around. Trixie slowly turned around.

Lou was standing near the garden, holding two treats.

The rabbit was immediately forgotten.

They raced back. Boomer sat. Trixie sat. Both looked up at Lou with perfect little innocent faces.

Lou shook his head. “You two are supposed to protect the garden.”

Boomer ate his treat. Trixie ate hers.

Then Boomer looked toward the fence. The rabbit was sitting on the other side, watching them.

Boomer gave one quiet little “woof.” The rabbit didn't move.

Trixie leaned toward Boomer. “Don't do it.”

Boomer thought about it. Then he looked at Lou. Then at the rabbit. Then back at Lou.

“Okay,” Boomer decided. “But tomorrow… we patrol earlier.”

And from that day forward, Boomer and Trixie became the official Garden Patrol. Trixie handled surveillance. Boomer handled chasing. And Lou handled the treats.

Lou and Peg and the Perfectly Ordinary Day

A Lou, Peg, Boomer & Trixie Story · September 6, 2026

One morning, Lou looked out the window and announced, “Peg, I don’t think we have much to do today.”

Peg looked at him. Then she looked toward the garden. Then toward the two dogs waiting by the door. Then back at Lou.

“Sure, Lou.”

That should have been his first warning.

After breakfast, Lou headed outside to check the garden. He was only going to be gone for five minutes.

An hour later, Peg found him standing among the vegetables, studying one plant as if it had personally asked him for advice.

“What are you doing?”

“Just checking things.”

Peg smiled. She knew what “checking things” meant.

Soon there were vegetables to pick, blueberries to inspect, a plant that needed tying up, another that needed watering, and three completely unrelated projects Lou suddenly remembered.

Meanwhile, Boomer and Trixie had appointed themselves supervisors.

Trixie followed Peg carefully through the yard. Boomer followed Lou. At least until he discovered something more important.

A squirrel.

“Boomer!” Lou called.

Boomer stopped. The squirrel stopped. Lou waited. Boomer looked at Lou. Then at the squirrel. Then back at Lou.

For perhaps the first time in Boomer’s life, good judgment won. He trotted back.

“Well, look at you,” Peg said. “He’s learning.”

“I’ve been working with him,” Lou said proudly.

Peg smiled. “Of course you have.”

By afternoon, the four of them were tired. Lou and Peg sat outside together while Boomer and Trixie stretched out nearby.

The garden was quiet. The sun was getting lower. There was no machine running, no dog barking, and, amazingly, Lou wasn’t planning another project.

For about thirty seconds.

“You know what I was thinking?” Lou said.

Peg laughed before he could finish.

“What?”

“We could put something over there.”

“Lou.”

“Just a small project.”

Boomer lifted his head. Trixie opened one eye. Peg shook her head and smiled.

After all their years together, she knew something very important.

There was no such thing as a small project when Lou started with, “You know what I was thinking?”

But that was part of their life together. A garden that was never quite finished. A house always filled with plans. Two little dogs following them everywhere. A family they loved. And Lou and Peg, side by side through all of it.

Lou looked across the yard. Peg looked at Lou.

“So,” she said, “what exactly were you thinking?”

Lou smiled. And Boomer and Trixie got up.

Apparently, the workday wasn’t over after all.

The Great Kitchen Caper

Boomer & Trixie Adventure · September 6, 2026

It was a quiet afternoon at Lou and Peg’s house.

Too quiet.

Trixie was curled up comfortably, minding her own business.

Boomer was not.

Boomer had discovered something. There was food on the kitchen counter.

He stood underneath it, staring upward.

Trixie opened one eye.

“Boomer.”

Boomer ignored her.

“Boomer, don’t.”

Boomer looked at Trixie. Then he looked at the counter.

Trixie knew that look.

“You’re gonna get in trouble.”

Boomer began investigating.

First, he tried standing on his back legs. Not tall enough.

Then he tried jumping. Definitely not tall enough.

Then Boomer noticed a chair.

Trixie sat up. “Oh, no.”

Boomer pushed against the chair with his nose. It moved. Just a little.

Boomer pushed again.

Trixie stared at him. “Boomer, this is a terrible idea.”

Boomer pushed harder.

SCRAAAAAPE!

The chair moved across the kitchen floor.

From the other room came Lou’s voice. “What was that?”

Boomer froze.

Trixie looked directly at him. “I told you.”

Footsteps were coming.

Boomer abandoned the mission and ran.

Trixie stayed exactly where she was.

Lou walked into the kitchen. He looked at the chair. He looked at the counter. Then he looked at Trixie.

Trixie sat perfectly still with the face of a dog who had spent the entire afternoon obeying every rule ever created.

“Where’s Boomer?”

Trixie slowly turned her head toward the hallway.

Lou followed her gaze.

There was Boomer. Only his little face was sticking around the corner.

“Boomer…”

Boomer disappeared.

Peg walked in. “What happened?”

“I think Boomer was trying to get onto the counter.”

Peg looked toward the hallway. Boomer peeked around the corner again.

Trixie looked at him. That look said everything.

“You’re in trouble.”

Lou tried to look stern. Peg tried to look stern.

Boomer slowly walked into the kitchen with his head down. Then he sat. He looked at Lou. He looked at Peg. And finally he looked at Trixie.

Trixie turned her head away. She wanted absolutely no part of this.

Lou finally started laughing. Peg did too.

Boomer’s tail began wagging. He had survived.

Then Lou moved the chair back to the table. Boomer watched carefully.

Because Boomer had learned an important lesson that day.

Not that stealing food was wrong. Not that climbing onto the counter was dangerous.

No.

Boomer had learned something much more useful.

Next time… move the chair quietly.
